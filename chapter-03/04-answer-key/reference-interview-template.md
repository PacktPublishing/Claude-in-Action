# Reference interview template: Marketing Specialist

This is a finished example of what you produce near the end of Chapter 3, when you save the reusable skeleton back into the project. It sits in the answer-key folder because you are meant to build your own first and compare.

Once you have a version you are happy with, yours or this one, it goes into `02-project-context/` so every future chat in the project can read it.

The reusable part of the guide for this role. Ten questions derived from the role, each with a four-level rubric. No candidate appears in this file, which is why it can live in the project context and be reused for every hire.

What changes per candidate is the probe under each question. Those are generated from the CV in the chat and are never scored.

**Role priorities:** 1. Paid campaign management. 2. Marketing analytics. 3. Email lifecycle.

---

## Behavioral questions

### B1. Priority 1

**Question.** Tell me about the last paid budget you were responsible for. How much, across which channels, and what did you change about how it was split?

- **4** Names the budget, the channels, and at least one reallocation decision, with the reason and what it was based on.
- **3** Names the budget and channels and describes managing them, but the reallocation sounds routine rather than decided.
- **2** Describes working on campaigns inside someone else's budget, or cannot say what the budget was.
- **1** No budget responsibility, or the answer stays at the level of platform features.

### B2. Priority 2

**Question.** Describe a time the numbers told you to stop doing something that was working, or that everyone assumed was working.

- **4** Names the metric, what it showed, who disagreed, and what happened after the decision.
- **3** Names the metric and the decision, without the disagreement or the follow-through.
- **2** Describes reporting the numbers and letting someone else decide.
- **1** No example, or an example where the decision came from opinion.

### B3. Priority 3

**Question.** Walk me through an automated email flow you built or improved. What was the trigger, what was in it, and what moved?

- **4** Describes the trigger, the sequence, the tool, and a before-and-after number.
- **3** Describes the flow and the tool, with the result stated loosely.
- **2** Contributed to a flow someone else owned, or reported on email performance without building.
- **1** No flow experience, or describes sending campaigns rather than automated flows.

### B4. Ownership and communication

**Question.** How do you report performance to people who do not work in marketing? Give me the last example.

- **4** Describes a specific audience, what they needed to decide, and how the reporting was cut to that.
- **3** Describes a regular report and its contents, without connecting it to a decision.
- **2** Describes sending a dashboard or a numbers export.
- **1** Reporting was somebody else's job, or the answer is about tools rather than people.

## Technical questions

### T1. Priority 1

**Question.** You have 8,000 euros a month across Google and Meta and a target on cost per lead. How do you decide the split in the first month, and what would make you change it in the second?

- **4** Gives a starting split with a reason, names what they would measure, and names the threshold that would move budget.
- **3** Gives a split and a measurement plan, without a threshold for acting.
- **2** Talks about testing both channels without a way to decide between them.
- **1** No method, or a split asserted with no reasoning.

### T2. Priority 2

**Question.** Last month your Meta campaigns show a strong return on ad spend and your Google campaigns look weak, but total revenue is flat. Walk me through how you would work out what is actually happening, and what you would change.

- **4** Names a concrete way to test the overlap and a specific budget change, with a reason.
- **3** Reaches a sound decision but does not say how they would confirm the cause first.
- **2** Talks about attribution models correctly but gives no example of acting on the data.
- **1** Repeats terms like multi-touch without explaining what they would do.

### T3. Priority 2

**Question.** A colleague says our signup numbers in GA4 do not match the numbers in the product database. Where do you start?

- **4** Names two or three plausible causes and how to tell them apart, and says which source they would trust for which question.
- **3** Names causes and a checking approach, without separating what each source is good for.
- **2** Would escalate to someone technical, with some idea of what to ask.
- **1** Treats the two numbers as interchangeable, or has no starting point.

## Situational questions

### S1. Priority 1

**Question.** Two weeks into the month, half the budget is spent and cost per lead has doubled. It is Thursday afternoon. What do you do?

- **4** Acts first to stop the spend, then diagnoses, and says what they would tell the team and when.
- **3** Diagnoses sensibly and acts, without urgency about the money still going out.
- **2** Investigates and reports, waiting for a decision.
- **1** No sense of what to do first, or blames the platform.

### S2. Priority 3

**Question.** Nobody currently owns our three email flows and they have not been touched in a year. Your first month: what do you do with them, and in what order?

- **4** Starts by measuring what the flows currently do, then names which one to fix first and why.
- **3** Proposes a sensible order of work without measuring first.
- **2** Proposes rewriting the emails, with no reference to performance.
- **1** No plan, or treats the flows as a copywriting task only.

### S3. Ownership and communication

**Question.** You believe we should cut a channel that the founder personally likes. How do you handle that?

- **4** Brings evidence, proposes a test rather than a verdict, and names what would change their own mind.
- **3** Brings evidence and makes the case, without a path for disagreement.
- **2** Would raise it and defer to the founder.
- **1** Avoids it, or would cut the channel without telling anyone.

## How to use this file

Add it to the project context, named for the role. Then, for each new candidate, start a chat in the project and write:

```
Here is the next candidate for the Marketing Specialist role. Use the
interview template in the project context, and generate the probes
for this CV.
```

You get the same ten questions and the same rubric, with a new set of probes. Read the probes before the interview: if they come back generic, the template is fine and the CV simply did not give much to push on, which is worth knowing in advance.
