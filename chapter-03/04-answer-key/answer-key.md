# Answer key

Read this after you have run the three candidates, not before.

Your output will not match word for word, and it should not. What should match is the shape: the knockout verdict, which priorities come back strong or thin, and whether the guide gets written at all.

## Candidate 2: David Okafor

**Expected verdict: strong fit. All knockouts pass. Full guide written.**

| Priority | Expected mark | Evidence in the CV |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Paid campaign management | Strong | Around 60k a month across Google Ads and Meta Ads, full responsibility for spend and results |
| 2. Marketing analytics | Strong | Cut cost per acquisition 28 percent by reallocating on GA4 and the attribution model, not last-click; set up GA4 for two clients |

The probes should point at his specific figures: the 28 percent, the five flows, the 60k budget. A probe that says "ask about his paid experience" is too soft, and the chapter shows how to fix that.

**One thing worth noticing.** David managed a 60k monthly budget and Brightpath's is 8,000. That is not a mismatch to screen out, but it is a real interview question, and a good guide raises it: ask what changes when the budget is one seventh the size and every test costs a bigger share of it.

**What the guide should do.** Mark priority 3 thin and quote the line that says she did not own the flows. Under the decision rule in the scorecard and decision framework file, a strong candidate with one thin priority is a conditional hire, so the interview exists to settle one question: can she own lifecycle email, or ramp into it fast enough?

That should show up in the situational questions. S2 in the template, the three unowned flows, is the question her interview turns on, and the probe under it should reference her reporting-seat experience specifically rather than asking about email in general.

**Why this candidate is in the pack.** Most real candidates look like Priya, not like David or Lena. The guide earns its keep here, because a generic interview would either reject her for the gap or hire her without testing it.

## If your output looks different

**The guide wrote questions for Lena.** The knockout step is being skipped. Confirm the instructions text is in the custom instructions field, not in the project context, and that the role success profile is in the context.

**Every question mentions the candidate by name.** The questions are being derived from the CV instead of the role. Check that the prompt says to derive all ten from the role, and that the custom instructions repeat it.

**Levels 2 and 3 of the rubric read the same.** The rubric is collapsing the middle, which is the defect the chapter walks through. Ask for a separate description per level and name what has to differ between 3 and 2.

**A priority came back strong for Priya on email.** Claude is reading "supported the email program" as ownership. Point it at the line that says she did not own the flows and ask it to re-mark.
