# Sample combined trend view

What the combined section returned for Wildgrass. Three findings, in the order the board produced them.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wildgrass Yoga (us) | 1.46M | +4.29% | 6.0 | 39% long, 61% Shorts | 113,000 | 7.77% |

## A shared bet we are not making

All three competitors publish long video almost exclusively. Petra has posted no Shorts since July 1, Fern Flows none since April, and Moon & Mat exactly one in the window, a promo for a free challenge. Wildgrass runs 61% Shorts.

Cadence is not what separates them either. Fern Flows and Moon & Mat are within 40,000 subscribers of each other, Moon & Mat posts more often, 3.7 a month against 3.3, and Fern Flows grows seven times faster. Fern Flows skipped three weeks in August and September and still gained subscribers. Whatever drives growth in this niche, it is not frequency.

## Where we actually sit

Wildgrass leads the board on both reach measures. Views per video as a share of subscribers: 7.77% against 5.03%, 2.90% and 2.41%. Growth: +4.29% against +2.15%, +0.74% and +0.30%. The only channel with higher absolute views per video is Petra, which is 9.4 times our size.

The founders came to this board believing the channel was the problem. On these numbers it is the healthiest thing in the business.

## Where the gap looks like it is

Inside our own card, long videos reach 13.1% of subscribers and Shorts reach 4.4%, roughly three times the difference. The course call to action sits at the end of the long videos, and 61% of uploads are Shorts.

That points at conversion rather than reach: the audience is growing and most of what reaches them carries no offer. Public data cannot show clicks, sign-ups or purchases, so this is a hypothesis to test inside the business, not a finding the board can confirm.

One more thing the board surfaced: none of the three competitors publishes technique instruction. Petra does posture, Fern Flows does flexibility, Moon & Mat does mobility and stress release. Wildgrass's technique Shorts are the only ones of their kind on the board, which turns the question from "should we drop Shorts" into "should the technique content be long-form, where the offer lives."
