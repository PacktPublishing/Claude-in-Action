# Choosing your signals

## The three questions

**1. What decision do we make repeatedly?**

Wildgrass: what do we publish next. A software company: what goes on the roadmap, or how do we price. A recruiter: which roles to post for. Name one decision, not three.

**2. What observable competitor behavior feeds that decision?**

Anything a data provider can measure from outside: publishing patterns, pricing pages, job postings, review themes, ad campaigns, search rankings.

**3. At what rhythm does that behavior change?**

Match your checking rhythm to it. A pricing page rarely moves, so a daily check spends attention on nothing. Upload cadence can shift in a few weeks, so a quarterly check finds the change after it stopped being news.

Aim for four to six signals that pass all three. More than six and someone reads past fields every week.

## What Wildgrass picked, and why

| Signal | Why it passed |
|---|---|
| Upload cadence and format mix | Directly changes what Wildgrass publishes next, and the format split turned out to be the finding |
| Outlier videos | Shows what the audience wanted, not what the channel intended |
| Topic direction, last 90 days vs the 90 before | Shows where a competitor thinks demand is heading |
| Views per video relative to subscribers | Grip on the audience, which raw subscriber count hides |
| Growth velocity | Rate, not total. The total describes the past decade |

## What they left off, and why

| Rejected | Reason |
|---|---|
| Absolute subscriber count | You cannot act on a competitor being big |
| A single viral video | Tells you about one video |
| Like counts | Makes you feel behind without telling you what to change |
| Production polish | Same |

The test for every one of them: could this change our next three videos?

## Which signals the connector returns, and which Claude computes

vidIQ returns upload schedule and outlier videos as named results. Topic direction, the views-to-subscribers ratio, and growth rate are not single answers it hands back; Claude pulls recent videos and stats and does the comparison itself.

The underlying numbers are current and sourced, which is a reasonable basis for the comparison. The comparison is still Claude's work, so spot-check it the way you would check a colleague's math.

## What the board actually found, and why that matters for your signal list

Wildgrass picked these five expecting the board to explain weak reach. It showed the opposite: they lead the board on growth and on views per video as a share of subscribers.

That is not a failed board. It is the signal list doing its job, which is to answer the question you asked rather than the one you assumed. Two consequences worth carrying into your own list:

- **Pick signals that can contradict you.** Had Wildgrass tracked absolute subscriber counts, every card would have confirmed that bigger channels exist and nothing would have been learned.
- **The split inside a signal can matter more than the signal.** The format mix was on the list as a publishing question. Splitting views per video by format, 13.1% of subscribers for long videos against 4.4% for Shorts, is what pointed at conversion.
