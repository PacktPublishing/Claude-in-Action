# Answer sheet: replying to the interview

For when you document one of your own processes. Claude's questions will not match
these headings exactly, since it asks about the process you described. Use this as a
checklist of what a complete reply covers, answer in one message, and put the question
number in front of each answer so nothing gets matched to the wrong question.

Two rules for filling it in. Write "I don't know" where you don't know, since that
becomes a [TO CONFIRM] marker in the draft and a decision for the owner rather than a
confident-sounding guess. And write "it depends, and here is what it depends on",
since that is what becomes a decision point in the finished procedure.

## Intake

- What starts the process, and every route it can arrive by
- How you verify the trigger is valid before acting on it
- Where you file the incoming document, and the exact naming format
- What you read off it, and where you look when a detail is missing
- Whether the path forks by type of client, order, or request

## The record or the file you create

- What system it goes into, named the way your team refers to it in writing
- Every field you fill, including the ones that are mandatory
- Where each field's value comes from
- Who you check with before you commit it, and how
- Which cases skip this procedure entirely

## What you send, and to whom

- Which template, where it lives, and what you change in it each time
- How many versions of each attachment exist, and the rule for picking one

## The waiting

- Whether anything chases the other party, after how long, and how many times
- Where the outstanding items are tracked, and who else can see them

## Checking and completion

- What you verify about what comes back, and what you deliberately do not
- What you do when the wrong thing arrives, and whether any clock restarts
- The status you set, and whether setting it triggers anything downstream

## The long tail

- The part of this process that takes the longest
- Whether that part already has a document, or should become its own

## Handoff

- Who you tell, by what channel, and what they need from you
- Any date or commitment set by someone other than you, and who sets it
- How you know who the right person is when it varies

## Exceptions

- What happens when the process stalls and nobody replies
- What happens when the original input changes after you started
- What changes in your busiest month

## The two questions nobody asks

- Who does this when you are away, and answer honestly if the answer is nobody
- Who checks the finished work, and whether that person is you

## The second round

Expect one more round after your first reply, and expect it to be the useful one. The
questions in it come from your own answers rather than from your description, so they
land on the parts you had merged without noticing: a category that turns out to be two
categories, a clock whose start date you had never fixed, a step whose order you had
been doing both ways.

Three things worth watching for in your own second-round answers:

- A fork you described as one condition that is actually two. If a third case can fall
  between your two paths, write both conditions out separately in the Decision points
  section.
- A status field doing two jobs. If one value means both "the work is finished" and
  "the next person has been told", nobody can tell those states apart later.
- A step whose order you have been varying. Pick one, say why in a sentence, and let
  that sentence go into the document.

When the questions stop, say so explicitly and give the go-ahead to write. Claude will
keep interviewing as long as you keep leaving openings.
