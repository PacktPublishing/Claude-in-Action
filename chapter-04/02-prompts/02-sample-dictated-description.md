# Sample: a dictated description

This is the description Nadia dictated in the chapter, in full. It is here so you can
try the interview prompt without documenting one of your own processes first. Paste
prompt 1, then paste this underneath it.

It reads badly on purpose. Repetition, corrections mid-sentence, and things remembered
out of order are what a real dictated description sounds like, and none of it is a
problem, because the interview is what turns it into material.

Two things are deliberately missing, so the interview has something to find: the
chase-up emails, and who runs any of this when Nadia is away.

---

so when a client signs, the signed engagement letter usually lands in my inbox, sometimes Ella forwards it, then I create the client in our practice software, I send the welcome email with the document checklist, and then we wait, that wait is the worst part, sometimes it's two days sometimes it's three weeks and there's nothing in the system that tells you which one you're in

um, ok let me go back a bit. so the letter comes in signed, I save it in the engagement letters folder in Drive, I name it with the client name and the date, and I read it because I need the legal name off it, the legal name not the trading name, people get that wrong, and I need to know what services we actually agreed because that changes what job types I set up later

then the record, so I go into the practice software, new client, paste the legal name, put in the address and the contact, and if it's a company I have to put the registration number in too. oh and I usually check with Ella that nothing extra got promised on the call, because sometimes there's a whole thing that isn't in the letter

then the welcome email, that's a template, it's in the Templates folder, and I attach the checklist, and the checklist depends, there's more than one, I just know which one to grab

and then we wait for the documents to come back, and I tick them off, I keep a list on the client record, and when everything's in I set them to ready and I do the software setup, which is honestly its own whole thing, the deadlines, the job types, the recurring tasks, that takes longer than everything else put together

and then I tell Ella or whoever the client manager is that the file's ready and when the first job is

I think that's it. it's maybe forty minutes of actual work but it's spread over however long the client takes. two to four of these a month, more in January. and Tomás has done it with me four times now and he still books a call every time, which is why I'm doing this
