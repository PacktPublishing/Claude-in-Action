# Sample: answering the interview

These are the answers to Claude's questions about the description in
`02-sample-dictated-description.md`, in two rounds, the way the interview actually
runs.

Claude's questions will not be numbered exactly like these, and it will not ask all of
them. It writes its questions from what you said, so a question here that it never
asked is a question you can ignore, and a question it asks that is not covered here is
worth answering in your own words. Paste round one, wait for the follow-ups, then paste
round two.

Notice what the answers do with the things Nadia does not know. "I don't know" and
"mark that" are the useful answers, because they become [TO CONFIRM] markers in the
draft instead of confident-sounding invention. Notice also that the second round is
where the real structure appears: a fork that turned out to be two forks, a clock with
no start date, and a status field doing two jobs.

## Round one

```
Answers, by your numbers.

Intake

1. No difference in what I do. Ella forwards it when the client sends it to
her instead of to ops, that's all. The only thing is it sometimes sits in her
inbox a day or two first, so the date on the letter and the date I start can
differ.

2. Everything comes through the e-sign platform, so if it's in my inbox as a
completed envelope it's signed. What I actually check is that the name that
signed matches a name on the letter, and that the last page is there with both
signatures on it. If either is off, it goes back to the client manager and I
don't create anything.

3. Client legal name, then the date, so "Harrow Foods Ltd, 2026-05-11". If a
file with that name already exists it means the letter was re-signed, and I
add "(resigned)" with the new date. That's rare.

4. The e-sign envelope has the registered name, and if it still isn't clear I
look the company up on the public register. For individuals it's whatever is
on their ID, not what they call themselves in emails.

5. Yes, and this is the big one I glossed over. Companies registered for VAT
go down a different path than individuals. Different checklist, and there's a
whole VAT setup in the software that individuals don't get: the VAT number,
the scheme, the return frequency, first period end, and they go into the VAT
deadlines view. Individuals skip all of that.

Client record

6. Keep it generic, "the practice software". We only have one, everyone knows
what it means, and the vendor renames its menus every few months.

7. Legal name, trading name if there is one, client type (company or
individual), registration number for companies, VAT number and scheme if
they're registered, address, primary contact name, contact email, contact
phone, assigned client manager, agreed fee and billing frequency, and the
services in scope. There's now a mandatory tax ID field too.

8. Off the engagement letter normally. If it's missing, the public register.
I don't ask the client for it, it's faster to look it up.

9. A message on Slack, not a call, so there's a record of the answer. If she
says something extra was promised, I don't put it in the record myself. She
either gets it added to the letter or she confirms in writing what the scope
actually is, and I attach that to the client record. Otherwise we've got a
file that doesn't match the contract.

10. Renewals and upsells don't go through this at all. The record already
exists, so it's just a scope change on the existing client. This procedure is
for brand new clients only.

Welcome email and checklist

11. Two. One for VAT-registered companies, one for individuals. The rule is
just the client type from the letter. The company one asks for more, mainly
the VAT registration certificate and the last filed return.

12. I change the greeting and the first line, then leave the rest. If the
client came in through a referral I add a sentence naming who referred them.

The wait

13. There are reminders, and I never told anyone about this. If the checklist
is still incomplete after five business days I send a reminder email from the
Templates folder listing only what's missing. If a second reminder five days
after that gets nothing, I escalate to the client manager and they call.

14. On the client record. I log one line per expected document off the
checklist, then tick each one off with the date it arrived. It isn't a
separate list, which is why nobody else can see where a client is stuck.

Doc tracking and readiness

15. I check they're legible and that they're the right document for the right
entity, not that the contents are correct. That's the job team's problem
later, not onboarding's.

16. It doesn't go on the record. I tell the client which document is actually
needed, and the five-day clock restarts from the date I told them. The one
that happens most is a personal ID sent for a company engagement.

17. It's a status field, and I set it to "awaiting documents" first and
"ready" when everything's in. Whether "ready" triggers anything else in the
software, I don't know. I've always assumed it doesn't. Mark that as something
to check.

Software setup

18. From the services in the letter. Each service maps to a job type. If the
letter covers something the software has no job type for, I stop and ask the
managing partner, because the job type is what generates the deadlines.

19. Mixed, and I can't give you the full rule. The statutory ones the software
calculates from the year end. The internal ones I set by hand, usually two
weeks before the statutory date. How it derives the VAT dates I honestly don't
know. Mark that one, and put the whole deadline question in the setup
procedure rather than this one, because it's the longest part of the job.

20. Monthly, quarterly, or annual. Bookkeeping clients are monthly, VAT
clients get the quarterly cycle on their return frequency, and everyone gets
the annual accounts task. I pick from the services and the VAT frequency, not
from anything the client asks for.

21. Nothing written down, I do it from memory every time, and it takes longer
than everything else in this procedure put together. It should be its own
document, so end this one at "complete the software setup by following
SOP-004" and we'll write that separately.

Handoff

22. A message on the client record so it's in the thread, not an email.

23. The client manager sets it, from the agreed start date in the letter and
whatever's free in the job calendar. I don't decide it, I just pass it on.

24. The engagement letter names the client manager. Ella is the managing
partner and she's the manager for most clients, but not all, so I read the
letter rather than assuming.

Edge cases

25. After the second reminder and the client manager's call, it goes to the
managing partner and it becomes a commercial decision. I've never had one go
past that.

26. If the letter is amended and re-signed, the new letter replaces the old
one in the folder, I re-read it for scope changes, and if anything material
changed I flag it to the client manager. Anything already set up in the
software stays, I only adjust what the change touches.

27. January is the same procedure, just slower. Everything takes longer to
come back and the reminders do more work. Nothing changes in the steps.

Two things you didn't ask about.

Nobody creates client records when I'm away. Not "someone covers", nobody. It
just waits until I'm back. That needs a decision from the managing partner,
so mark it rather than filling it in.

The other thing is who does the checking at the end. Right now I check my own
work, which is fine when it's me, and it's exactly the thing that will go
wrong when it isn't.
```

## Round two

```
1. Third path, and I'd never separated it out until you asked. A company
that isn't VAT-registered gets the company checklist, because I still need
the incorporation documents and the registration number, but it skips all of
step 6, the VAT number, the scheme, the frequency, the deadlines view. So the
fork is on two things, not one: client type picks the checklist, VAT
registration picks whether the VAT setup runs. Individuals are always the
individual checklist and never the VAT setup.

2. Before, and it should be before, though I've done it both ways. If she
tells me the scope is different from the letter, I don't want a record already
sitting there with the wrong services on it, because the services are what the
job types come off. So: read the letter, check with Ella, then create the
record.

3. From the welcome email sent date. The letter arrival doesn't start
anything, since the client doesn't know what to send until the checklist
reaches them. If I send the email late, the clock is late too, and that's
correct.

4. Free text today, which is the honest answer and also why the handoff is the
part that goes missing. It should be both: the message on the record plus the
status set to ready. Treat the status change as the thing that counts as done,
and the message as the notification. That way there's a field somewhere that
says whether the handoff happened.

5. Same sources as the legal name. For companies it's on the engagement letter
or the public register. For individuals it's the tax reference off their ID or
their last return, and that one does sometimes have to come from the client,
in which case it goes on the checklist as an outstanding item like any other
document and I don't leave the field blank and forget it.

6. It stops entirely, and the re-request isn't mine. I send it back to the
client manager with what's wrong, in writing, and they own getting a corrected
envelope out. Nothing gets created in the software in the meantime, no record,
no checklist, nothing, because a half-created client with no valid contract is
worse than an empty inbox. When the new envelope lands it starts at step 1
again, saved with the new date.

Two more things surfaced while answering.

On 4, if the handoff status is what counts as done, then the "ready" status
and the handoff status can't be the same field. Ready means the documents are
in. Handed off means the client manager has been told. Right now I use one
status for both, which is probably why nobody can tell the difference.

On 1, if the fork is really on two conditions, the checklist and the VAT
setup, then the decision points section needs both written out separately,
not one combined "is it a VAT company" question. Otherwise the non-VAT
company falls between the two paths, the way it just did.

Go ahead and write it.
```
